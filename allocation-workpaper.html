Partnership Capital-to-Tax Allocation Workpaper

An Excel workpaper that ties each investor's capital account to their share of the fund's tax figures, built for more than 200 limited partner accounts.

The problem

Investment funds set up as partnerships don't pay tax themselves. Instead, the fund's income, gains, and losses are allocated to each investor, and every limited partner needs their share reported correctly. That means each partner's capital account has to be connected to their slice of the tax numbers, and with hundreds of partners, doing it by hand is slow and easy to get wrong.

What I built

  • A capital-to-tax allocation workpaper covering more than 200 limited partner accounts.
  • XLOOKUP formulas that pull each partner's figures from the source data automatically, instead of copying numbers by hand.
  • IFNA wrappers so a partner who is missing from a source returns a clean value instead of an #N/A error that would break the totals.

Teaching it to the team

I prepared and presented an internal training deck that walked through how partnership funds are structured and how P&L gets allocated to partners, so others could understand and use the same approach.

Related research

During the same internship, I researched IRS Forms 1065 (partnership returns) and 1120 (corporate returns), along with their supplemental schedules, for a managing director's technical review.
