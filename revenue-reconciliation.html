Restaurant Revenue Reconciliation

Making sure the sales a busy restaurant records actually match the money it takes in, across every way customers order and pay.

2,200+orders per month

The problem

A restaurant takes money through several routes at once: cash, credit cards, and orders coming in through different channels. The point-of-sale (POS) system records what was sold, but those records only mean something if they match the cash in the drawer and the card payments that actually settle. When they don't match, someone has to find out why.

What I do

  • Reconcile POS-reported cash flows against the cash actually collected.
  • Match credit card batches to the card sales recorded in the POS.
  • Review adjusted revenue edits so every change to a recorded sale is accounted for.
  • Validate more than $130,000 in gross revenue each month this way.

Running the operation behind the numbers

The reconciliation sits on top of a high-volume operation that I help run. I manage seven fulfillment channels handling more than 2,200 orders a month, optimized seating for an 88-seat dining room to keep tables turning, and supervise a 12-person team to resolve service issues and keep standards consistent.

Why it matters for accounting

This is the same core skill behind a bank reconciliation or a general-ledger review: confirming that every record of a transaction agrees, and tracking down the reason when it doesn't. Doing it every month with real money has made accuracy a habit.
